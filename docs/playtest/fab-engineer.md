# Fab engineer, not a gamer

## Where I would quit, and why
Seed 777 (NMOS lot), step 17: the activation anneal. It ran by itself and took 4.8 points, the biggest single loss of the run. There was no chart, no card and no reason given. The gate oxidation before it did the same thing (3.3 points). Then the diagnosis told me the lot was hurt by "handling particles": 0.3 to 1.0 points smeared over every step, on a wet-clean chart that never tripped a rule. At that point I was sure the game decides the answer and doesn't show me the evidence. If I can't diagnose the loss, I'm watching a progress bar. I finished the run because a colleague asked me to. On my own I'd have closed the tab there and not started a fourth run.

## Problems
1. **The diagnosis asks for evidence the game never shows** — "Read the evidence: yield by step, the wafer map and the tool charts." Then every answer depends on things I never saw. Keyhole void: "opens on narrow vias only", on a trench level with no vias, with no cross-section and no void count. The plating chart was "in control" the whole time. Dishing: "wide lines thin and high-resistance", but I was never shown a line resistance. Native oxide: "C-V shows a thicker electrical oxide", but there is no C-V anywhere. I got Dishing right only by elimination: I knew what I hadn't done. I got the other two wrong while my reasoning was sound: the biggest-loss step, plus a chart that had been trending. A new hire learns that diagnosis is guessing what the game rolled. Evidence: seed 123456 step 22 (docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/123456-40-s123456-29-diagnosis.png), seed 777 step 19 (docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/777-55-s777-22-diagnosis.png), seed 42 step 10.
2. **"In control" charts that turn out to be drifting** — On seed 777 the deposition chart was green, with no badge on the floor plan and no chance to PM before the room. Then 2.7 of the room's 3.2 points were blamed on "the Deposition tool's drift". Almost every room also says "0.1 to 0.6 points came from drift" on charts the game itself calls in control, including one PM'd one step earlier. The glossary promises that drift "shows on SPC before the lot gets there". Teaching "the chart said in control, but you lose to drift anyway" teaches a new hire that SPC doesn't work. Evidence: seed 777 step 4 (docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/777-08-s777-05-padox-spc.png), seed 123456 steps 4 and 12.
3. **Card numbers contradict the card's own physics** — "Trim dose from the SPC trend" says it "only fixes a steady shift, not scatter". It still promises 0.3 to 0.4 points on a perfectly centred tool (seed 42) and on a tool I had just PM'd (seed 123456). On a just-PM'd tool, using the old trend over-corrects in real life. "Timed over-etch" says "on a steady tool the attack costs about what it saves", then saves 1.4 points on a centred STI etch. The STI trench is etched into silicon, so the "film below" doesn't exist there. A player learns "always apply APC, always over-etch". That's the opposite of what the text says, and the opposite of how a fab works. Evidence: seed 42 step 5 (docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/42-11-s42-04-litho.png), seed 777 step 8 (docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/777-19-s777-09-sti-etch.png).
4. **Big losses with no lever and no explanation** — Plating (4.4 to 4.7), Cu CMP (6.0 to 6.3), gate oxidation (3.3), activation anneal (4.8) and barrier/seed (3.3) all run by themselves: "Nothing could be played here". Diffusion and implant have no SPC chart at all. PMing plating and CMP before they ran moved their loss by about 0.3 points. These rooms are where I have the strongest real-world opinions, and they are dead air. Evidence: docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/123456-32-s123456-22-plating.png, docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/777-51-s777-19-anneal.png.
5. **Wrong shop talk copied across flows** — The Cu-line Q-time says "native oxide keeps growing", but the trench bottom is oxide and there's no silicon there. The real worry is copper or low-k. The NMOS rinse card still says "where the barrier goes on next", in a flow with no barrier. One "Deposition" tool with one particle chart does CVD oxide and the PVD barrier/seed (Cu line), and pad oxide/nitride and poly (NMOS). Those are different tools. "Seam rate on fill monitor (%)" is not a term anyone uses. The last room is called "Metrology" on the map and "Electrical test" in the step name. A fab person winces at each one. A new hire repeats them in a meeting. Evidence: seed 123456 step 15 (docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/123456-26-s123456-18-qtime-corridor.png), seed 777 step 2, seed 123456 step 13.
6. **Rework card that can never be played** — "Strip and re-expose" sits in every litho hand, greyed: "Needs a litho step just before this one. No step on this route does that." I'm standing in the litho bay, one step before etch. It never becomes playable on either route, not even at gate litho where rework matters most. It contradicts the route on screen and made me doubt I understood the map. Evidence: docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/123456-14-s123456-11-litho.png.
7. **Events that teach the wrong idea of how a fab is run** — In the quality audit, walking the auditor through gets a wet bench "serviced", and "ask them to come back later" is offered as an option. Auditors don't fix tools, and you don't dodge them. In the supplier change, "switch now" to an unqualified slurry mid-lot is offered as a fair tradeoff, when it's a change-control violation. The hot-lot request came up five times in three runs, twice in one lot, so it feels like the game has three events. Evidence: docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/777-06-s777-03-event.png, docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/123456-34-s123456-24-event2.png.
8. **The wafer is a square, and "risk" has no unit** — The map is a 5x5 square grid, so the corners sit off a round wafer. That's the first picture a new hire sees. Q-time "oxide risk +0.040" has no unit. The hot-lot event translates its risk into "about 2.0 points", but the Q-time stop doesn't, so I couldn't weigh it. The lot cost starts at 2000, which is never shown. The hot-lot fee is subtracted from lot cost. Evidence: docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/123456-03-s123456-03-room1.png, docs/playtest/shots/fab-engineer/123456-26-s123456-18-qtime-corridor.png.

## Compliment
The STI CMP room on seed 777 names the cause (dishing in wide trenches), says which earlier choice (dummy fill) already shrank it, and offers a sensible knob (low-pressure polish). The SPC charts I checked by eye (9 points in a row, one-sided particle spec, two-sided CD spec, the excursion text) are all correct.

## Ratings (1 = bad, 5 = great)
- Clarity: 3 — The glossary, the charts and the map averaging to the line yield are honest, but "drift" on in-control tools, unitless risk and greyed-out cards with contradictory reasons kept me second-guessing.
- Real decisions: 2 — The PM choice from charts is a real decision, and so is the STI dishing chain. Half the yield loss is in rooms with nothing to play, and the cards' numbers ignore what the charts say.
- Wanting another run: 2 — I'd come back for more rooms like STI CMP. I won't come back to be told I misdiagnosed something I was never shown, and I would not give this to a new hire in its current state.
